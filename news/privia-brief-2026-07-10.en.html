Implications for PRIVIA

  • Membership/subscription is now the dominant model — validated, but increasingly contested. Medical Economics and HLTH confirm that subscription-based concierge care is becoming mainstream and expanding into specialties. PRIVIA's recurring-revenue model is on the right side of history; its defence is continuity of family medical care, not the subscription itself, which is becoming commoditised.
  • The Gulf already has bricks and mortar, not just capital. Six Senses AMAALA + Saudi wellness partnership + UAE commitment to ‘connected intelligence’. For potential international expansion, the Gulf now offers UHNW demand, state backing and physical infrastructure already under way.
  • Institutional capital is buying private healthcare in Europe. Blue Owl moves into Spire (UK) and funds consolidate in Spain (reported yesterday). PRIVIA will compete and/or negotiate with better-capitalised players: it would be wise to decide soon whether the strategy is to remain independent, seek a partner or be open to acquisition.

  • The ‘lack of trust in public healthcare’ driver remains active in Spain and the UK. NHS consultants' strike action (UK) and Quirónsalud's paid-for health checks (Spain) point to the same shift towards premium private healthcare. A sustained macro tailwind for the PRIVIA thesis.
