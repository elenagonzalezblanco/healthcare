Implications for PRIVIA

  • The Gulf has moved from capital to bricks and mortar — and local competition is emerging. Longevium (three Dubai clinics), Aldar+PureHealth (longevity communities) and Brainify.AI (Riyadh) are building a real physical offering. For PRIVIA, the UAE/Gulf is both the most attractive international market and one where local incumbents are already staking out positions: entering early or through a partnership is becoming more valuable.
  • Rivals are heavily capitalised: the test is becoming commoditised, but the relationship is not. Neko Health raises $700m for AI body scanning. PRIVIA cannot compete on hardware/marketing spend; its moat is long-term continuity of family medical care and carefully curated coordination, not the one-off test.
  • Demographics are the ultimate tailwind. A 12% decline in the EU population by 2100, Hong Kong as a hub and a $6.8tn wellness market. Structural demand for preventive healthcare and continuity of care is assured for decades — reinforcing PRIVIA's long-term thesis.
  • In Spain, the incumbent is moving closer to ‘family care’. Adeslas is promoting health through a family lens, while healthcare inflation puts pressure on insurers. PRIVIA must protect the differentiation of its family-office model through clinical depth and genuine coordination before the major players claim the narrative.
